How to Choose Receipt Paper Size: 2¼" vs 3⅛"

By Performance Supply Depot | January 2024 | 4 min read

Ordered the wrong paper size? It won't fit. Here's what you need to know: two widths (2¼" and 3⅛") dominate the market, each fits specific printer types, and one simple measurement tells you which you need. This guide shows you how to pick correctly every time.

The Problem: Paper Size Confusion Costs You Money

Most receipt paper returns happen for one reason: wrong width. You ordered 2¼" paper, but your kitchen printer needs 3⅛". Or vice versa. Either way, you've got a case of paper you can't use and a printer that still needs rolls.

Here's why this happens so often:

  • Printer manuals use different terms (58mm vs 2¼", 80mm vs 3⅛")
  • Online listings show sizes in inches and millimeters interchangeably
  • Some businesses have multiple printer types and mix up orders
  • "Standard size" means different things to different vendors

⚠️ The Cost of Getting It Wrong

A case of 50 thermal rolls costs $80-$120. Returns take time. Meanwhile, you're out of paper during business hours. Getting the size right the first time saves money and headaches.

Which Printers Use Which Size?

2¼" (58mm) - The Compact Choice

This smaller width fits portable and compact printers. You'll find it in:

  • Clover Mini and Clover Flex
  • Square Terminal and Square Register
  • Mobile printers: Epson Mobilink, Star SM-T300
  • Older terminals: VeriFone Vx520, Ingenico series
  • Credit card machines: Most standalone terminals

Best For:

  • Food trucks and mobile vendors
  • Counter-service restaurants
  • Retail with minimal item detail
  • Customer-facing receipts only

3⅛" (80mm) - The Kitchen Standard

The wider format is standard for full-size receipt printers. You'll find it in:

  • Epson TM-T88 series (T88V, T88VI)
  • Star TSP100 and TSP650
  • Clover Station and Station Pro
  • Kitchen printers: Epson TM-U220, Star SP700
  • Full-service POS systems

Best For:

  • Full-service restaurants
  • Kitchen tickets with modifiers
  • Itemized receipts with 20+ lines
  • Businesses needing duplicate copies

The 30-Second Identification Method

Don't know which size you have? Here's the fastest way to find out:

  1. Look at your current roll. Remove it from the printer if needed.
  2. Measure the width. Use a ruler or tape measure across the face of the roll.
  3. Compare:
    • About 2¼ inches (58mm) = 2¼" size
    • About 3⅛ inches (80mm) = 3⅛" size

Still Not Sure? Check These Places:

  • Printer model number: Look on a sticker on the printer
  • Original packaging: Previous paper orders show the size
  • POS settings: Many systems show printer specs in settings

Core Size Matters Too

Width isn't the only spec. The core (the cardboard tube in the center) must match your printer's holder:

  • ½" core: Fits most compact and mobile printers
  • 1" core: Fits older terminals and some credit card machines
  • 1⅗" core: Fits full-size printers with large-capacity holders

Common Mistake: Core vs. Width

A 3⅛" paper with a ½" core won't fit a printer designed for 3⅛" with a 1⅗" core. Check both specs before ordering.

Length: How Much Paper Do You Actually Get?

Roll length determines how often you change rolls. Here's what California restaurants typically choose:

  • 85' rolls: Compact machines, lower volume (Clover Mini)
  • 230' rolls: Standard kitchen printers, good for 1-2 weeks
  • 273' rolls: High-volume locations, fewer roll changes

California Business Examples

Here's what similar businesses in California use:

  • San Francisco food truck: 2¼" x 85' for Square Terminal
  • LA full-service restaurant: 3⅛" x 230' for kitchen tickets
  • Bay Area coffee chain: 2¼" x 230' for customer receipts + 3⅛" x 230' for kitchen
  • San Diego retail boutique: 3⅛" x 230' for detailed itemized receipts

Summary: How to Choose Receipt Paper Size

  • 2¼" (58mm): For Clover Mini/Flex, Square Terminal, mobile printers, credit card machines
  • 3⅛" (80mm): For Epson TM-T88, Star TSP100, Clover Station, kitchen printers
