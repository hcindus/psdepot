How Many Pourers Do You Need?

Count your well bottles. Each bottle that's poured regularly needs a Capton pourer. A standard 6-bottle well needs 6 pourers. Add pourers for your rail bottles, back bar premium bottles used for highballs, and your house wine. A typical full-service bar needs 12-24 pourers.

Small Bar (1 station, 6 wells)

Start with 6 × 1.5 oz pourers for your well. If you have a wine program, add 2 × wine pourers. If you do cocktails, add 2 × 1 oz pourers for vermouth, bitters bottles, and specialty liqueurs. Budget: ~$200 for 10 pourers.

Full Bar (2-3 stations)

The Capton Variety Pack (SKU CAP-VARY-12PK) is the smart buy — you get 3 of each size. Buy two packs for a 3-station bar. Budget: ~$560 for 24 pourers covering all stations.

Restaurant with Bar Program

Well: 1.5 oz pourers on all rail bottles. Wine station: 5 oz wine pourers on house wines. Tasting/cocktail station: 1 oz pourers on specialty bottles. Premium well: 2 oz pourers for rocks pours and martinis. The Variety Pack covers all of this in one order.

ROI: How Fast Do Capton Pourers Pay for Themselves?

Over-pouring by just 1/8 oz per drink costs $3-8 per bottle, depending on your pour cost. A bar pouring 50 drinks per night loses $150-400/month to over-pouring. Six Capton 1.5 oz pourers cost $150 and eliminate that loss immediately — ROI is typically 2-4 weeks. That's before you account for the improved consistency and customer experience.
