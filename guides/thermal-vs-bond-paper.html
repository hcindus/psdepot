When to Choose Thermal Paper

Thermal paper is the right choice for the vast majority of modern businesses. It's faster, quieter, and requires no ribbon changes — you just drop in a new roll when it runs out. Our 3 1/8" x 230' thermal rolls (SKU 54-230) work with virtually all modern POS printers from Epson, Star, Bixolon, NCR, and POS-X. The only real downside of thermal paper is that receipts can fade over time — especially if stored in hot environments or exposed to direct sunlight. For most retail transactions, this isn't a concern because customers don't typically keep receipts for years. If archival durability matters (legal, medical, or warranty records), thermal may not be the best fit. For businesses wanting thermal convenience without BPA/BPS, our PF-230 phenol-free paper is the answer.

When to Choose Bond Paper

Bond paper and impact printers serve specific use cases where thermal can't compete. The biggest advantage: multi-part carbonless printing. If you need to print a receipt and a duplicate simultaneously — one for the customer and one for your file — impact printing with 2-ply carbonless paper (SKU CC-235) is the solution. This is why restaurant kitchens, auto repair shops, and businesses with paper filing systems often keep impact printers running alongside modern thermal systems. Bond paper is also more durable for long-term storage — it won't fade over decades the way thermal can. If you're running an Epson TM-U220 or similar impact printer, you'll need both our 13-305 bond paper and ERC ribbons. Check our printer compatibility guide to confirm your printer model.

How to Tell Which Type Your Printer Uses

The simplest test: look at the printer when it prints. If it moves silently and the paper comes out dark, it's a thermal printer. If it makes a clicking/buzzing sound and you can see a ribbon cartridge inside, it's an impact printer using bond paper. Also check the paper itself: thermal paper has a glossy, slightly waxy feel and often turns black if you scratch it with a fingernail. Bond paper feels like regular office paper — matte and fibrous. Still unsure? Remove the roll and check the printer model number, then look it up in our printer compatibility guide.

Can You Use Thermal Paper in an Impact Printer — or Vice Versa?

No. They are completely incompatible. Putting thermal paper in an impact printer will result in faint or blank output because there's no heat source to activate the coating. Putting bond paper in a thermal printer produces nothing at all — bond paper has no thermal coating. Using the wrong paper type won't damage your printer, but it won't print anything useful. Always match your paper type to your printer technology.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is thermal paper more expensive than bond paper?

Per-roll, yes — thermal paper typically costs more. However, when you factor in the ongoing cost of replacement ribbon cartridges ($42 each for our SKU 62245) and the labor of changing ribbons, the total cost of ownership is often lower with thermal. Most businesses save money switching to thermal over a 2-3 year period.

Do I need to keep both types of paper in stock?

Only if you have both thermal and impact printers in your business. Many restaurants, for example, use thermal printers at the front counter and impact printers in the kitchen (for durable, heat-resistant receipts). In that case, you need thermal paper for the front and bond paper + ribbons for the kitchen. Our bulk ordering guide helps you stock both efficiently.
