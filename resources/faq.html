POS Supplies FAQ

Answers to the most common questions about thermal paper, receipt rolls, printer ribbons, and POS consumables.

What businesses use thermal paper?

Virtually every business with a point-of-sale system uses thermal paper. Restaurants print kitchen tickets and customer receipts. Retail stores print sales receipts. Hotels print folios. Medical offices print patient receipts. Coffee shops, bars, grocery stores, auto shops — if there's a POS terminal or credit card machine, there's thermal paper inside it.

What size receipt paper do I need?

The two most common sizes are 3 1/8" x 230' (for full-size POS printers like Epson TM-T88, Star TSP100, Clover Station) and 2 1/4" x 74' (for credit card terminals like Verifone, Ingenico, Clover Mini). Check your printer model or measure your current roll. Our Receipt Paper Size Guide has full details.

Which POS paper works with my printer?

Most thermal printers use 3 1/8" (80mm) rolls. Credit card terminals use 2 1/4" (57mm) rolls. Impact/dot-matrix printers need bond paper instead of thermal. Check our Printer Compatibility Guide for a full breakdown by brand and model.

How long do thermal receipts last?

Standard thermal paper receipts typically remain legible for 3-5 years under normal storage conditions. Heat, sunlight, and contact with plastics (like vinyl wallets) can accelerate fading. For longer archival needs, consider bond paper or carbonless copies. We also carry phenol-free thermal paper with improved durability.

Warehouses typically need durable thermal transfer labels that can withstand temperature changes and handling. We stock a range of barcode labels and ribbons compatible with Zebra, Datamax, and Sato printers. Call us with your printer model for a match.

How often should I replace receipt paper?

You replace it when the roll runs out — there's no scheduled replacement cycle. A typical restaurant goes through 1-2 rolls of 3 1/8" thermal paper per day per terminal. Retail stores vary widely by transaction volume. Keep 2-4 weeks of backup stock on hand to avoid running out.

What is the difference between thermal paper and bond paper?

Thermal paper uses heat-sensitive coating — the printer's thermal print head creates the image without ink or ribbons. Bond paper (also called impact paper) is standard paper used in dot-matrix printers that strike through an ink ribbon. Thermal is more common in modern POS systems; bond paper is used in older kitchen printers and some back-office applications. Read our full comparison.
